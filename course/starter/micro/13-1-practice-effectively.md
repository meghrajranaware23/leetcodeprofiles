## Part 1 — Effective Practice Planning

### 1. Why planning beats random Easies

Problem hopping feels like progress but scatters attention. A weekly plan keeps you in one family long enough to recognize it — and reserves time for re-solves.

### 2. The skill in one sentence

> **Mon–Thu new (same family) · Fri re-solve · Sat timed · Sun plan** — repeat weekly.

### 3. Weekly Practice Plan Template

| Day | Focus | Problems |
|-----|-------|----------|
| Mon–Thu | New Easy, same family tag | 1/day from course |
| Fri | Re-solve 1 wrong | Mistake journal pick |
| Sat | Timed 20 min (Day 14 prep) | 1 Easy cold |
| Sun | Review + plan next week | 15 min journal |

### 4. Climbing Stairs — seed only

`ways(n) = ways(n-1) + ways(n-2)` — feels like recursion/DP.

Trace n=3 on paper:

```
n=1 → 1 way
n=2 → 2 ways (1+1 or 2)
n=3 → ways(2)+ways(1) = 3
```

> **Full treatment:** Recursion & Backtracking or DP Ascension packs. Today: trace n=3 on paper only.

### 5. What strong planners do

| Weak habit | Strong habit |
|---|---|
| Random problem hopping | Same family tag Mon–Thu |
| Treat Climbing Stairs as full DP lesson | Trace seed only; defer to Ascension |
| No Friday re-solve slot | Block 20 min Friday in calendar |
| No Sunday plan | 15 min: pick next week's family |

### 6. The key insight

> **Consistency beats intensity** — 20 minutes daily with a plan beats three-hour random binges that don't repeat.

### 7. Common Day 13 mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Random problem hopping instead of weekly plan | Write Mon–Sun plan in journal |
| Treated Climbing Stairs as full DP lesson (over-studied) | Trace n=3 only; pack later |
| No Friday re-solve slot scheduled | Block Friday now |
| Skipped Roman as string-parse practice | Tag string-parse before coding |
